02

The one blocked thing, and what we know

DRONE_B cannot keep a USB WiFi adapter alive. What the kernel said, in its own words:

from B's dmesg
usb 1-1-port4: Cannot enable. Maybe the USB cable is bad?
usb 1-1-port4: attempt power cycle
usb 1-1-port4: unable to enumerate USB device
usb 1-1.4:    ath9k_htc: Firmware - htc_9271.fw download failed

Three facts pin this down:

  • The same adapter model works perfectly in DRONE_A, on the same software, at full IBSS. So the software and the configuration are not at fault.
  • Both of your adapters now fail in B. When two different adapters fail in one node, the node is the common factor, not the adapters.
  • A 50 KB firmware transfer failed. That is a tiny transfer. A USB link that cannot carry it is marginal at the electrical level.
This is a hardware fault. Do not debug it in software

Nothing in the repository will fix this, and every hour spent reading logs is an hour not spent on the report. Section 3 is a bounded 30 minute triage with a hard stop.

Part two · What to do

03

B triage: 30 minutes, then stop

Work down this list. Stop at the first step that gives you a stable wlan1. If you reach the end, stop anyway and go to section 4.

Step 1: is the port dead, or just weak? (5 min)

This is the single most useful test and almost nobody thinks to do it.

on DRONE_B
# Put a USB memory stick or a keyboard in the SAME port the
# adapter was using. Then:
$ lsusb
What the answer means
Stick appears
The port works. It cannot supply enough CURRENT for the adapter, which draws 400 to 500 mA. This is a power problem. Go to step 3.
Stick does not appear
The port itself is dead. Go to step 2.

Step 2: try every other port (5 min)

The Pi 4 has four. You have been using one of them. Try the adapter in each of the other three, waiting 10 seconds after each, and run lsusb each time. The two blue USB 3.0 ports are worth trying first.

Step 3: change the power supply (10 min)

Not the cable to the adapter. The supply feeding the Pi.

Use the best supply you have, ideally an official Raspberry Pi 4 supply or anything rated 3 A at 5 V. Swap it in, reboot B, and check:

on DRONE_B
$ iw dev wlan1 info
$ vcgencmd get_throttled

# get_throttled: anything other than 0x0 means the Pi has been
# under-volted. That is a direct confirmation of the diagnosis.

Step 4: swap the whole node (5 min)

Take the SD card out of B and put it in DRONE_S's Pi, with a known-good adapter. If it works there, B's Pi board or its USB controller is at fault and you have found it. If it fails there too, look at your supply again.

Hard stop at 30 minutes

If none of that gives you a stable wlan1, stop. Order a powered USB hub, which takes the adapter off the Pi's power budget entirely and is the definitive fix. Then go straight to section 4 and get on with the report. A second node is not needed for any of today's work.

What to ignore for now

Things that look like problems and are not. Do not spend time on any of these.

Looks alarmingActually
htc_9271-1.4.0.fw failed with error -2Normal. The driver tries a versioned filename, fails, and loads htc_9271.fw. Your firmware is installed and fine
sudo dtn-doctor: command not foundOnly installs on the next setup_node.sh run. Use sudo bash tools/dtn_doctor.sh from the repo root
"no peers visible" on a healthy nodeCorrect when the other node has no radio. One node can never show a peer
No sync lines in the logFollows from no peers. It is not a missing table; the loop has nobody to talk to
DTN_MAC and DTN_MAC_ALT in the confsNo longer used. Interfaces match by driver now. Leave them alone
DRONE_S being untouchedFine. Nothing in the next two weeks needs it
Country code showing 00 not LKCosmetic for now. Note it before the range measurements, since it caps transmit power, but it does not block anything
If you are unsure whether something is a real fault, run sudo bash tools/dtn_doctor.sh and trust its verdict. It now knows about the flapping-adapter case that fooled it earlier, so a clean result from it genuinely means clean.
